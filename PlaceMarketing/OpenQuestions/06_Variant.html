Question 1 · 4 marks
Resource Management and Territorial Vocation According to Caroli: Examine the three resource management strategies (optimization, partial modification, radical change). Explain in which contexts a "Radical Change" is necessary and how the attraction of external resources must be balanced with the anchoring of existing ones.
Sample answer

Caroli (1999) — three ways to work on territorial resources / vocation:

1) Optimization — keep the current vocation and make it stronger. Use what you already have better (skills, firms, infra). Classic first strategy in the MCQs: reinforce the existing territorial vocation, don’t throw it away for fashion sectors.

2) Partial modification — vocation stays recognisable, but you adapt pieces of it (new services around the old industry, related diversification). Not a full identity reset; more like evolution.

3) Radical change — when the old vocation is exhausted or the context collapses (industrial crisis, obsolete agriculture, place stuck). Then you redesign strategy toward new assets and new actors (e.g. agriculture → tourism / services — same logic as “change vocation” in the governing-in-turbulence paper).

Radical change needs external resources (investors, new knowledge, companies), otherwise you can’t jump. But if you only import and ignore what already exists, you lose anchoring: people, genius loci, local skills, social capital. Then the new vocation feels fake and stakeholders don’t follow. So: attract outside, but root the new path in what the place can still integrate — otherwise you get artificial positioning / propaganda, not a viable system.

Question 2 · 4 marks
The Role of Events in Place Enhancement: Analyze the classification of events (Mega, Special, Hallmark, Community) and their experiential impact. How can an event become a driver of internal development and attraction of external investment without compromising the authenticity of the territory?
Sample answer

From the Sansone–Bruni events paper (Roche / Hall table):

Mega — Olympics, World Cup, Expos: global audience / global TV, huge resources, short high-profile impact on image (Hiller). Special — e.g. F1 Grand Prix, world regional sport: national/international media. Hallmark — big city festival / national sport with consolidated fame: more regional, prestige already attached to the place. Community — rural town / local events: local press, local public — smaller scale, strong for community bonds.

Experiential impact matters more than size alone: what people feel and remember, plus socio-economic effects (tourism, culture, business, politics). A small community event can still generate value if it works as experience; a mega event can fail if planning is weak.

Driver of internal development + external investment: paper’s point — events push tourism economy, reception services, partnerships, knowledge sharing, can attract external activities. But authenticity: don’t paste a random mega format that ignores local identity. Link event to vocation / genius loci, involve local firms and citizens (meta-management / public–private), plan long-term benefits not only one weekend of visitors. Same rule as branding: substance + local meaning, or you get overtourism noise and a place that doesn’t recognise itself.

Question 3 · 4 marks
Smart City and Big Data as Co-creation Tools: Explain how a "Smart City" is not merely a technological infrastructure, but a vital service system. How can the use of Big Data and Open Data improve market knowledge and foster democratic citizen participation (Nudges)?
Sample answer

Smart City ≠ sensors for the sake of sensors. In the course: technology is a tool to optimise co-creation of value and wellbeing of citizens — not the final goal, not a substitute for politics. Closer to a viable service system / servicescape: actors (residents, firms, municipality) interact through enabled services; tech multiplies interaction and resource integration (service science line in the territory/place papers).

Big Data — useful because they improve understanding of social dynamics and markets through flow analysis (mobility, use of spaces…). Help strategy, but they don’t replace talking to people and don’t auto-create Place Image.

Open Data — make information accessible so citizens and firms can use it; supports transparency and citizen-sourcing / co-design (democratic participation), not only internal municipal dashboards.

Nudges — gentle informational pushes toward virtuous behaviour (recycle more, soft mobility…), not fines and not TCM “orders”. With open/shared data you can design better nudges because you know how people actually move and choose. Together: Smart City as living service system — data for knowledge + open access + soft stimuli — so tech stays inside value co-creation, not one-way “smart” propaganda.
