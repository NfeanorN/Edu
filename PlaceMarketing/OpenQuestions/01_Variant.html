Question 1 · 4 marks
The ontological evolution from "Territory" to "Place": Analyze the fundamental distinction between the objective view of territory and the phenomenological view of place. Using the metaphors of the "House" and the "Home", explain how the emergence of a place depends on the subjective interpretation of the observer and the integration of tangible and intangible resources.
Sample answer

In the course paper that uses house/home for this, territory is the objective thing: a spatial entity under a public administration — boundaries, ownership, government, population. You can point it on a map. Place is the phenomenological side: a system of values / subjective reading of that territory, closer to what Cresswell calls the “realm of meaning” (what people do, say, feel about the locale), not only materiality (buildings, topography, admin borders).

House = structure you can describe (shape, size, address, who owns it). Home = immaterial / emotional — perceptions, values, sentiments that the same building generates for the people living it. Same split: territory ≈ house; place ≈ home. Place doesn’t “exist” just because the municipality drew a line. It emerges when subjects experience the territory and recognise some benefit from a territorial value proposition.

Also from that paper: place emerges as a systemic entity through the observer’s perception (sense, feel, act, react) and through resource integration between actors and the territory. Tangible / operand / “hard” side (infrastructure, factors of production…) mixes with intangible / operant / “soft” side (skills, knowledge, identity, attachment). Without that interaction you’re still looking at territory. With it, place shows up — and the boundaries of the place value proposition can even be fuzzy, because they depend on perception, not only on the admin map.

Question 2 · 4 marks
The role of "Genius Loci" and "Vocation" in territorial positioning: Define the concepts of Genius Loci and Vocation of a territory. Discuss how these intangible components influence the strategic formulation of a Place Brand identity, preventing the risk of standardized or artificially constructed positioning.
Sample answer

Genius loci (Norberg-Schulz and how it’s used in the enterprise–place papers) = spirit of the place. Not a logo. It’s that concentration of entrepreneurial culture, history, traditions, local values, knowledge, creativity… the stuff that made Italian districts hard to copy even when production can move abroad. Soil and buildings are supports; genius loci is what makes the place specific.

Vocation is more strategic: what the place is oriented to / good at, and what actors try to optimise or even change. In the “governing in turbulence” framework, place marketing value-co-creation driven is literally about integrating stakeholders’ resources to optimise the place vocation — or shift it (e.g. from agriculture toward services/tourism) if that’s the strategy. So vocation isn’t only “we have always done X”; it’s the direction actors work on together.

For Place Brand identity this matters because identity (Anholt / Kapferer line in the SIM paper) is what the “owner” side sets — meaning, intention, vocation — while image is what recipients keep in their heads. If identity starts from genius loci + a real vocation, you avoid the empty competition for uniqueness (every city slogan with “unique / smart / innovative”). Kavaratzis also pushes that branding isn’t inventing associations in a lab; it grows from place-making elements and from people interacting with the place. Artificial positioning looks nice on a poster and then nobody recognises it on the ground. Genius loci + vocation keep the brand tied to something actors can actually integrate resources around.

Question 3 · 4 marks
Service Dominant Logic (S-D Logic) applied to Place Marketing: Describe the transition from the traditional "Goods-Dominant" view to the "Service-Dominant" perspective in the territorial context. In particular, focus on the concepts of "Value-in-use", "Value-in-context", and the role of stakeholders as "co-creators" rather than passive consumers of the territorial offer.
Sample answer

Goods-dominant logic = place almost like a product: hard resources / operand stuff (land, facilities…) get packaged and “sold” to tourists or investors. Value is treated as embedded in the output and transferred at exchange. Classic place promotion thinking.

S-D logic (Vargo & Lusch — and how Warnaby / Polese apply it to places) flips it. What gets exchanged is mainly applied knowledge and skills (operant resources), not goods. FP6: the customer / user is always a co-creator of value. FP7: the enterprise (here: the locale / government) cannot deliver value, only offer a value proposition — people may take it up or not. Value-in-use = value appears when people actually use / experience the place by applying their own operant resources, not when the brochure is printed.

Value-in-context (Chandler & Vargo; also in the place-brand SIM paper): same resources don’t give the same value for everyone — it depends on the network of actors, institutions, culture, the specific situation. So a square isn’t “worth” the same for a resident, a tourist, a firm. Stakeholders stop being targets (operand) and become operant resources / co-creators: they integrate resources with others toward benefits (wellbeing, quality of life in the co-creation papers). Place marketing then isn’t “we promote → you consume”; it’s setting conditions so actors can interact and co-create the place value proposition.
