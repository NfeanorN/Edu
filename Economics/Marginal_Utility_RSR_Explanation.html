Marginal Utility & Rational Spending Rule

Source: Robert H. Frank, Microeconomics and Behavior — Ch. 1 (marginal benefit, Figure 1.1) and Ch. 3 (rational consumer choice, Appendix, Figure 3.15). Shelter & food example: M = $100/wk, PS = $5/sq yd, PF = $10/lb.

Exam questions covered:
  1. Define total and marginal utility (enjoyment / satisfaction).
  2. What is the economic meaning of MU / P?
  3. Define the rational spending rule (RSR). What should a rational consumer do if the actual allocation does not comply with RSR?

1. Total utility (TU) / Совокупная полезность

Total utility is the overall satisfaction, enjoyment, or benefit that a consumer receives from consuming a given quantity of a good or a bundle of goods.

Совокупная полезность (TU) — общее удовлетворение от потребления заданного количества товара или набора товаров.

Frank (Ch. 3, Appendix) assigns a number U (utils) to each bundle. Example utility function for food F and shelter S:

U(F, S) = F × S (utils per week)

  • F = 4 lb, S = 3 sq yd → U = 12 utils
  • F = 8 lb, S = 6 sq yd → U = 48 utils (higher total utility)

As you consume more (moving to higher indifference curves), total utility rises — but each extra unit typically adds less than the previous one (diminishing marginal utility).

2. Marginal utility (MU) / Предельная полезность

Marginal utility (MU) is the extra satisfaction (enjoyment) from consuming one additional unit of a good, holding other consumption constant.

Предельная полезность (MU) — прирост удовлетворения от ещё одной единицы товара.

MUx = ΔU / Δx   (or ∂U/∂x for small changes)

For U(F, S) = F × S:   MUF = S,   MUS = F

At (F=4, S=3): MUF = 3, MUS = 4.

Diminishing marginal utility / Убывающая MU

Typically, each successive unit adds less extra satisfaction than the previous one. Frank (Ch. 1, Figure 1.1): the marginal benefit of talking on the phone falls as you talk longer — same idea as diminishing MU for goods.

Обычно каждая следующая единица добавляет меньше удовлетворения, чем предыдущая.

TU rises but at a decreasing rate; MU falls as quantity increases.

Water vs diamonds (exam intuition)

Water has high total utility (life depends on it), but the marginal utility of the next glass is low when you are not thirsty. Diamonds have high MU per unit but a very high price. Rational choice compares MU per euro, not raw MU alone.

3. Economic meaning of MU / P

MUx / Px = marginal utility per euro spent on good x

“Bang for the buck”Extra satisfaction from the last € spent on good x / полезность на последний €

At optimumAll goods have the same MU/P = λ (marginal utility of income) / на оптимуме MU/P одинаковы
Link to Ch. 1For the consumer, MB of the last unit = MU; MC of buying it = P → optimum where MU = P in marginal terms, or MU/P = 1 per good in common units
You do NOT maximize MU of one good. You equalize MU/P across all goods. High MU alone does not mean “buy more” if the price is also high.

4. Rational Spending Rule (RSR) / Правило рациональных расходов

A rational consumer with a fixed budget allocates spending so that the last euro spent on each good yields the same marginal utility:

MUx / Px = MUy / Py = … = λ

Plus: budget is fully spent (for an interior optimum on the budget line).

Frank (Ch. 3) — shelter S and food F:

MUS / PS = MUF / PF

Equivalently at the optimum (Figure 3.15): MRSS,F = PS / PF = 5/10 = 1/2

Geometrically: the best affordable bundle is where the budget line is tangent to the highest reachable indifference curve (Frank, Figure 3.15).

5. If allocation does NOT satisfy RSR — what to do?

Case A: MUx/Px > MUy/Py

Good x gives more satisfaction per euro than good y.

Rational action:

  1. Buy more x → MUx falls (diminishing MU)
  2. Buy less y → MUy rises
  3. Continue reallocating until MUx/Px = MUy/Py

Case B: MUx/Px < MUy/Py

Rational action: buy less x, more y, until MU/P is equalized.

Frank numeric example (Ch. 3)

Suppose at the current bundle:

MUF / PF = 8 / 10 = 0.8

MUS / PS = 3 / 5 = 0.6

0.8 > 0.6 → food gives more utils per dollar → buy more food, less shelter until the two ratios are equal.

Textbook (Figure 3.15): at a point on the budget line where IC slope (MRS) = 1/4 but budget slope = PS/PF = 1/2, the consumer should trade shelter for food until MRS = 1/2.

6. Exam trap — “When is MU maximized?”

Do not confuse three different ideas:

  • MU of one good — highest on the first unit; then falls (diminishing MU). You do not “maximize MU” in the consumer optimum.
  • Total utility U — maximum when MU = 0 (one more unit adds nothing; beyond that MU < 0 and U falls).
  • Consumer optimum with a budget — not max MU, but equal MU/P across goods + budget exhausted.

Short exam answer (EN)

Total utility is total satisfaction from a consumption level. Marginal utility is the extra satisfaction from one more unit. MU/P is marginal satisfaction per euro — the return on the last euro spent on each good. The rational spending rule requires MUx/Px = MUy/Py = λ with the budget exhausted. If MUx/Px > MUy/Py, buy more x and less y until ratios are equal; if MUx/Px < MUy/Py, do the opposite.
