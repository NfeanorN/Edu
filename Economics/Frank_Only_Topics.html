1. Budget Constraint

Frank Ch. 3 · Rational Consumer Choice · Eq. (3.1)–(3.2) · ~pp. 54–57

Definition

The budget constraint shows all bundles of two goods the consumer can afford when spending all income. Bundles on or inside the budget triangle = affordable (feasible) set.

Eq. (3.1)
PS·S + PF·F = M

Shelter example: S = shelter, F = food, M = income.
General form: PX·X + PY·Y = M
Eq. (3.2) — line form (F on vertical axis):
F = M/PF − (PS/PF)·S

Vertical intercept: M/PF
Horizontal intercept: M/PS
Slope = −PS/PF (negative price ratio = opportunity cost)
Slope = rate at which you can trade one good for the other at market prices. Example in Frank: PS=$5, PF=$10 → slope −1/2 → opportunity cost of 1 sq yd shelter = 1/2 lb food.

Shifts (book)

ChangeWhat happens to the line
Price of horizontal good risesRotates inward about the vertical intercept (slope steeper)
Price of horizontal good fallsRotates outward about the vertical intercept
Income rises / fallsParallel shift out / in (slope unchanged)
Both prices × same factorParallel shift (like an income change); relative prices unchanged

Composite good

When there are many other goods, Frank puts “all other goods” on the vertical axis as the composite good Y with price $1. Then slope = −PX.

Kinked budget (Example 3.1)

Quantity discount (Gigawatt Power): different prices for different ranges of kWh → kinked budget line (not one constant slope).

Бюджет: весь доход тратится на два блага. Наклон −PX/PY. Цена одного блага → поворот вокруг другой оси. Доход → параллельный сдвиг.

2. Intertemporal Budget Constraint

Frank Ch. 5 · pp. 146–147 · Figs. 5.13–5.14 · Eq. (5.4)

Idea

Same as a budget line, but the two “goods” are current consumption C1 and future consumption C2. You can save or borrow at interest rate r.

Endowment always affordable: (C1, C2) = (M1, M2)

Max future if save all today: C2max = M1(1+r) + M2
Max today if borrow all future income: C1max = M1 + M2/(1+r)
Present value of future income (5.4):
PV(M2) = M2 / (1+r)

Intertemporal budget (PV form):
C1 + C2/(1+r) = M1 + M2/(1+r)

FV form:
C1(1+r) + C2 = M1(1+r) + M2

Slope = −(1+r)
Giving up $1 of C1 today buys (1+r) of C2 tomorrow.
Horizontal intercept = present value of lifetime income = M1 + M2/(1+r).
Left of endowment on the line = saver; right = borrower.

Frank numerical example (p. 146): M1=50,000, M2=60,000, r=0.20 → C2 = 120,000 − 1.2 C1, or 1.2 C1 + C2 = 120,000. Figure 5.13 slope = −1.2.

Межвременной бюджет: C₁ и C₂ вместо двух товаров, «цена» сегодняшнего потребления — (1+r). Наклон −(1+r). Страницы книги 146–147.

3. Price Elasticity of Demand

Frank Ch. 4 · ~pp. 103–110 · point-slope method

Definition (Frank)

Price elasticity of demand = percentage change in quantity demanded that results from a 1% change in price.

ε = (%ΔQ) / (%ΔP)

If price rises 1% and quantity falls 2%, ε = −2.
Frank’s wordingCondition
Elasticprice elasticity < −1 (e.g. −2, −3…)
Inelasticprice elasticity > −1 (e.g. −0.5; closer to 0)
Unit elasticprice elasticity = −1
Frank keeps the negative sign. Many exams also use |ε|. Elastic means |ε| > 1.

Point-slope method (Frank)

ε = (P/Q) × (1 / slope of demand)
For a linear demand, slope is constant, so elasticity changes along the curve (high |ε| at high P / low Q; low |ε| at low P / high Q).

Elasticity and total expenditure / revenue

Frank links elasticity to how total spending responds to a price change:

  • Elastic demand: price cut → total expenditure rises
  • Inelastic demand: price cut → total expenditure falls
  • Unit elastic: total expenditure roughly unchanged

Determinants (Frank Ch. 4)

  • Availability of substitutes (more substitutes → more elastic)
  • Budget share (larger share → more elastic)
  • Time horizon (longer time → more elastic)
  • Necessity vs luxury

Also in Ch. 4: income elasticity and cross-price elasticity (substitutes vs complements).

PED = %ΔQ / %ΔP. У Frank знак отрицательный. Эластичный: ε < −1. Связь с выручкой: на эластичном участке снижение цены увеличивает TR.

4. FC, VC, TC, AFC, AVC, ATC, MC

Frank Ch. 9 · Costs · ~pp. 266–277 · Zara’s Cleaners · Eq. MC = w/MP

Short-run setup (Frank)

Capital is fixed in the short run; labor is the usual variable input. Example: Zara’s Cleaners — bags of laundry with labor L and fixed capital.

SymbolNameFrank meaning
FCFixed costCost of fixed factors; does not vary with Q in the short run. FC = r·K0
VCVariable costCost of variable factors (usually w·L)
TCTotal costTC = FC + VC
AFCAverage fixed costAFC = FC / Q (always falls as Q rises)
AVCAverage variable costAVC = VC / Q = w / AP (when only labor varies)
ATCAverage total costATC = TC / Q = AFC + AVC
MCMarginal costExtra cost of one more unit: MC = ΔTC/ΔQ = ΔVC/ΔQ
When labor is the only variable factor and wage w is fixed:

MC = w / MPL
AVC = w / APL

So: MP rises → MC falls; diminishing MP → MC rises (U-shaped MC).

Shapes (Frank)

  • AFC: always declining (same FC spread over more Q).
  • AVC and ATC: typically U-shaped.
  • MC: typically U-shaped when diminishing returns set in.
  • Vertical gap ATC − AVC = AFC (shrinks as Q grows).
  • MC crosses AVC and ATC at their minimum points.
  • Min AVC occurs at a smaller Q than min ATC (because falling AFC keeps ATC declining a bit after AVC turns up).

Constant-MC special case (Example 9.1)

Production Q = 3KL with K fixed at 4, w=$24, r=$2 → TC = 8 + 2Q → MC = 2 (flat). This is constant returns to the variable factor — no diminishing returns region in that example.

U-shaped MC ↔ diminishing returns with fixed capital (short run).
Flat MC ↔ constant MP of the variable factor (textbook special case), not “automatically long run.”
FC не зависит от Q. VC зависит. ATC = AFC + AVC. MC = w/MP. MC пересекает AVC и ATC в минимумах. U у MC — короткий период с убывающей предельной отдачей.
