Phillips Curve

Course textbook: Robert H. Frank, Microeconomics and Behavior, 7th ed. (micro only). The Phillips curve is not in Frank. This page follows Exam Prep — Topic 31 only — no extra formulas or invented examples.

Okun's Law (GDP growth vs. unemployment) is not in Frank and is not an exam topic in the Prep Guide — it is omitted here.

Short-run Phillips Curve

The short-run Phillips curve shows a negative (inverse) relationship between inflation π and unemployment u: π ↑ ↔ u ↓ (short-run trade-off).

Short-run Phillips: negative slope — lower u, higher π along the curve.

Long-run Phillips Curve

The long-run Phillips curve is vertical at the natural rate un. There is no permanent trade-off — only expected inflation πe shifts the short-run curve.

n SRPC₂ (πe↑)

SR negative slope; LR vertical at un; upward SR shift when πe rises.

Stagflation

Stagflation (1970s): supply shock (oil) → short-run Phillips shifts up → both π↑ and u↑. The data show positive correlation because the curve shifted, not because the original SR slope is positive.
