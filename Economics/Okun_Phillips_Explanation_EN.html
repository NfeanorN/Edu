Okun's Law & Phillips Curve

Textbook: Frank, Microeconomics and Behavior (micro only). Okun and Phillips are macro — not in Frank. Phillips follows Exam Prep Topic 31.

Okun's Law

In plain words: when GDP grows strongly, unemployment falls. In a recession, unemployment rises. Okun links Y and u — not inflation.

Okun's Law is an empirical relationship between real GDP and unemployment (Arthur Okun, 1960s US data).

Rule of thumb (1 to 2):

A 1 percentage point rise in unemployment → real GDP about 2% below potential (output gap).

(Y − Y*) / Y* ≈ −2 × (u − un)

Y* = potential GDP · un = natural rate · output gap = (Y−Y*)/Y*

Example: un = 5%, actual u = 7% → cyclical unemployment = 2 pp → output gap ≈ −2 × 2 = −4% (recession). Strong growth → u falls (boom).

Strong GDP growth → unemployment falls. Recession → unemployment rises.

Phillips Curve (Exam #31)

The short-run Phillips curve shows a negative relationship between inflation π and unemployment u: π ↑ ↔ u ↓ (short-run trade-off).

Short-run Phillips: negative slope — lower u, higher π along the curve.

Long-run Phillips Curve

The long-run Phillips curve is vertical at the natural rate un. There is no permanent trade-off — only expected inflation πe shifts the short-run curve.

n SRPC₂ (πe↑)

SR negative slope; LR vertical at un; upward SR shift when πe rises.

Stagflation

Stagflation (1970s): supply shock (oil) → short-run Phillips shifts up → both π↑ and u↑.
