Demand (Спрос) — Frank, Ch. 2

Source: Robert H. Frank, Microeconomics and Behavior, Chapter 2 (Supply and Demand), pp. 24–36. All examples below are from the textbook.

Источник: Frank, глава 2. Все примеры — из учебника (рынок лобстеров в Hyannis, Massachusetts).

What is Demand? / Что такое спрос?

The demand curve (D) is a “behaviour map of buyers”: it shows how much buyers want to buy at every possible price (Frank, Ch. 2, p. 24).

Кривая спроса D — «карта поведения покупателей»: сколько они хотят купить при каждой возможной цене.

  • Y-axis: price P ($/unit)
  • X-axis: quantity Q (units per day, etc.)

Law of Demand / Закон спроса

Lower price → buyers want more. When a lobster costs $20, only wealthy people buy. When it costs $4, everyone buys, even students for dinner. The D curve slopes down to the right.

Ниже цена → больше покупают. При $20 за лобстера покупают только состоятельные; при $4 — почти все. Кривая D идёт вниз вправо.

Movement along D vs. shift of D / Движение вдоль D и сдвиг D

Frank, Ch. 2, p. 36 — “What Shifts Supply & Demand”

Rule from the book:

  • A change in the good's own price → movement along the curve.
  • A change in anything else → shift of the curve.

Правило: изменилась собственная цена товара → движение вдоль кривой. Изменилось что-то другое → сдвиг кривой.

A demand curve is a “holding-everything-else-constant” graph: incomes, prices of related goods, season, etc. are fixed when we draw D.

Кривая D строится при прочих равных (ceteris paribus): доходы, цены связанных товаров и т.д. зафиксированы.

Movement along D — textbook example

The price of lobsters fell from $12 to $8. That is a change in the good's own price → movement along the same curve D (from Q = 3 thousand to Q = 4 thousand), not a shift.

Цена лобстеров упала с $12 до $8 — меняется только собственная цена → движение вдоль той же кривой D (с 3 до 4 тыс.), а не сдвиг.

Movement along D: P falls $12 → $8, Q rises 3 → 4 thousand (Figure 2.1 schedule).

Shift of D — textbook examples

Tourists' incomes rose → not the price of lobsters → D shifts right (new curve D₁).

Crabs (a substitute) became more expensive → D for lobsters shifts right (people switch from crabs to lobsters).

Доход туристов вырос → D сдвигается вправо. Крабы (заменитель) подорожали → спрос на лобстеров сдвигается вправо.

Shift right: more demand at every price (e.g. higher incomes, substitute pricier).

Shift left: less demand at every price (e.g. butter — complement — becomes more expensive).

Practice — movement or shift? (Frank, Ch. 2)

  1. The price of lobsters fell from $12 to $8. → Own price changed → movement along D.
  2. Tourists' incomes rose. → Other factor → D shifts right.
  3. Crabs (a substitute) became more expensive. → Other factor → D shifts right.
  4. The price of lobsters rose because of a storm. → The storm shifted S to the left; the price rose as a consequence. Along D we move (higher price → less demanded), but D itself does not shift.

Equilibrium — D and S together / Равновесие D и S

Frank, Ch. 2, p. 27 — Figure 2.5

In Figure 2.5, D and S for lobsters cross at P* = $8 per lobster and Q* = 4 thousand lobsters per day. At $8, quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.

В Figure 2.5: P* = $8, Q* = 4 тыс. лобстеров/день. Объём спроса = объём предложения.

Figure 2.5 — equilibrium in the lobster market: E at P* = $8, Q* = 4 thousand/day.

Summary (Frank, Ch. 2):

  • D slopes down: lower P → more Q demanded.
  • Own price change → movement along D.
  • Other factors (tastes, income, substitutes, complements, expectations, population) → shift of D.
  • Equilibrium: QD(P*) = QS(P*) — in the lobster example, P* = 8, Q* = 4.

Algebra (Ch. 2, p. 40)

If QD = a − bP and QS = c + dP, set QD = QS for equilibrium P* and Q*.
