Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF)

Source: Frank, Ch. 17 — «General Equilibrium and Market Efficiency». PPF built from an exchange/production economy with clothing and food.

1. What the PPF shows

The PPF shows the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce with fixed resources and technology. Points on the curve are efficient; inside = unused resources; outside = impossible for now.

2. Opportunity cost = slope of PPF

MRT (marginal rate of transformation) = slope of PPF = opportunity cost of one more unit of good X in terms of good Y.
Ch. 17: moving along the PPF between clothing and food, producing more food means giving up some clothing — the slope tells how much clothing is sacrificed per extra unit of food.

3. Efficiency condition (Ch. 17)

For an efficient mix of goods for society: MRT = MRS — the rate at which the economy can transform goods equals the rate at which consumers are willing to trade them (marginal rate of substitution from indifference curves).

Bow-shaped PPF: increasing opportunity cost as you specialize more in one good.

Frank derives the PPF from the Edgeworth box (Ch. 17) — same clothing/food framework, not abstract «guns and butter».
